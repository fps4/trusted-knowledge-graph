Working lab · Lab Firm LLP

A knowledge layer a lawyer can act on — and an AI can stand on

This is a running demonstration of one architecture for a law firm's knowledge: facts about matters, clients and people, joined across the firm's systems, that an assistant can answer from — while the firm's information barriers hold for every fact, every document and everything derived from them.

The problem it addresses

Questions search cannot answer

"Have we advised a Dutch fund manager on an AFM investigation since 2021 — who led it, what was the outcome?" needs a complete list, joined across practice management, CRM and HR. Top-k passages give a few lookalikes, presented as the whole answer.

Barriers that leak through derived content

A note filed on an open matter that cites a walled one passes any document-level filter. So do summaries, counts and extracted facts. Access has to follow lineage, not just the file.

Words with more than one meaning

"Active client" means four different things to Business Development, the practice groups, Finance and Risk. An assistant that picks one silently gives a confident, wrong number.

How it works

Every question — from the chat, the guided form, or an AI agent over MCP — goes through one resolver, as one person:

PlanMatch the question to a competency question the graph is built to answer.
TermsResolve business words against an owned glossary; ask when a word is ambiguous.
DecideCheck every candidate matter and fact, and what it derives from, against the barrier rules (OPA).
RetrieveOnly then query the graph and the search index — bound to what was allowed.
RecordWrite the decision, its rules and its grounds to a hash-chained record.

The graph's spine is mapped from systems of record (practice management, CRM, HR); facts from documents are extracted by Claude with a named source and a review state. The estate is invented: around 400 matters with their clients, people and documents.

The question battery is built from competency questions — the graph's home ground. Where search should win (exact wording, the long tail) is stated in the repository, not hidden.

Meet the people — open a screen

Each screen is one person, fixed. There is no login and no switcher, on purpose: a role an assistant can pass as a parameter is not a boundary.

A five-minute tour

  1. Mara asks the AFM question. Five matters, each with its source. Click the answer, then Sources to open a PDF and Lineage to see fact → document → matter. Note the one outcome that is still unconfirmed.
  2. Sanne asks the same question. Four matters. Explain shows the rule that withheld the fifth — B-03, its owner, the date it was set — and how much was blocked directly vs by lineage.
  3. Sanne asks “Who led M-2022-0117?” — refused, with the grounds. “Who has the most AFM investigation experience?” — refused as an aggregate, because a count can reveal a walled matter.
  4. Kim asks about “active client”. Four readings, four owners, four counts — the assistant asks which one instead of choosing.
  5. Risk opens the record: everything just asked is there, Sanne's refusals under B-03, and the chain verifies. Paste a trace id from any answer to see the same grounds, read back later.

On each screen

  • Chat — plain questions; Claude (Anthropic API) picks the competency question and calls the resolver as that person. Each tool call shows its outcome.
  • Guided — pick a competency question and fill its slots. No model and no network: the fallback that always works.
  • Inspector — for the selected answer: Trace (route, terms, slots, policy version), Explain (the rules), Sources (PDFs signed with that person's own credentials), Lineage, and Record (the stored decision).

What this is not

  • Not a real firm, and not production. A pattern, implemented at laptop scale and measured.
  • Identity is asserted by each screen's own key, not federated from an identity provider — the one place the lab is weaker than the design.
  • The record is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.
